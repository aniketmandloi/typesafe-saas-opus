# Polar is the billing provider, and seats stay out of the seam

Billing is [Polar](https://polar.sh), as merchant of record, behind the kit's billing adapter. The adapter is about ten Organization-keyed operations — `ensureBillingAccount`, `createCheckoutSession`, `getBillingPortalUrl`, `getSubscription`, `changePlan`, `setQuantity`, `cancel`, `resume`, `getEntitlements`, `verifyWebhook` — and **seat assignment is deliberately not one of them**.

**Organization-owned subscriptions are first-class, which is why Polar fits a B2B kit at all.** Polar's `Customer` is a discriminated union on `type: "individual" | "team"`, settable at creation, and the team model has three entities: Customer (the billing entity, owning subscriptions and payment methods), Member (roles `owner` / `billing_manager` / `member`, with *independent* benefit grants) and CustomerSeat (`pending` → `claimed` → `revoked`). Polar's own docs say to identify end users by their member identity rather than by the paying customer — which is exactly the Organization-owns-subscription, user-consumes-entitlement split this kit's tenancy model needs. An Organization maps to a `type: "team"` Customer keyed by `external_id`, and that key is **the Organization's id, never its slug**, because `external_id` is immutable once set.

**Seats stay out of the interface because the kit owns the roster anyway.** Membership and Role exist for RBAC and invites whatever billing does, so `setQuantity` is driven from roster size and mirroring members into Polar `Member`s is an adapter-internal detail. This is the load-bearing shape decision, not a tidiness one: seat occupancy is the single largest Polar-shaped concept, since Stripe models per-seat pricing as `quantity` alone and has no equivalent of invitations, claiming or revocation. Keeping seats out converts the largest swap cost into adapter-internal code; letting them in would make a swap a rewrite of the org model. The `FeatureKey` vocabulary is the kit's for the same reason — a Polar Feature Flag name must never reach an authorization check.

Per [ADR-0004](./0004-adapter-seam-demand-shaped-and-statically-composed.md) the billing seam is justified by **containment, not substitutability**: no Stripe adapter exists, and none will be written speculatively.

## Considered options

**Stripe** is the default assumption, and choosing it means **becoming the merchant of record**: tax registration per jurisdiction, Stripe Tax, remittance, invoicing. No adapter hides that, and for a kit whose audience is self-serve B2B it is the largest operational cost in the stack. Polar carries it for roughly 2.5–3× Stripe's rate — since the 2026 increase, 5% + 50¢ on the free tier down to 3.4% + 30¢ on Scale, plus 1.5% on international cards, $15 per dispute regardless of outcome, and transaction fees that are not refunded when an order is. Entitlements did not decide it either way: Stripe Billing has a real Entitlements product whose `active_entitlement` and summary webhook parallel Polar's customer state almost one to one.

**`@polar-sh/better-auth` is not adopted.** The published plugin (1.8.4) is *user-centric* — one Polar Customer per User, with the Organization present only as a `referenceId` in metadata — which is the wrong shape for this kit. It also declares a peer on `@polar-sh/sdk ^0.47.0`, which the current 0.49.0 does not satisfy, so a fresh install conflicts. The org-centric integration exists on `main` as 1.9.0 but is unpublished, marked `@experimental`, and its own changelog warns against enabling it in an app that already syncs org billing — which this kit does, by the paragraph above. The Organization↔billing sync is therefore the kit's own code behind the adapter, modelled on what 1.9.0 does; revisit the plugin when it ships non-experimental.

**The SDK is pinned at 0.49.x**, which is the deprecated line: `polarsource/polar-js` is archived, and its hand-written replacement is published only as a 1.0 alpha under the `next` tag. The choice is between a deprecated stable SDK and an alpha, and behind the adapter the 1.0 move is internal either way.

## Consequences

**The enterprise ceiling is capped structurally, not by effort.** Polar's `Order` is a transaction record, not an invoice-first payment request — no PO numbers, no net terms, no due dates, no send-an-invoice-to-be-paid flow. And under merchant of record the contract of sale is between the buyer and *Polar*, so a custom MSA or DPA with the kit operator as counterparty is impossible by construction. "Micro-SaaS to enterprise" means self-serve and mid-market here, not six-figure contract sales. Choosing Polar is choosing that ceiling.

**Webhook handlers refetch customer state and overwrite the local mirror, rather than applying event deltas.** Polar documents neither ordering nor idempotency; it retries up to ten times with exponential backoff, times out at 10 s (2 s recommended), and auto-disables an endpoint after ten consecutive non-2xx responses. Entitlements are gated on the mirror, never on a live call. Signature verification is Standard Webhooks for secrets created on or after 2026-09-08 and legacy Polar HMAC before, which the adapter absorbs.

**There are no `customer_seat.*` events** in the published catalogue — seat transitions surface only through `benefit_grant.*` and `customer.state_changed`. And revoking a seat does *not* reduce the billed quantity; that is a separate `seats` update. Both are drift the roster-driven `setQuantity` avoids by construction, which is a second reason the roster stays ours.

**Polar's API versions expire.** `YYYY-MM` versions ship each quarter with roughly a nine-month life, then are removed and return 404, and omitting `Polar-Version` silently rides Current. A forked project breaks on a timer unless it bumps — a fork-and-go hygiene obligation rather than an adapter concern.

**Sellers are limited to the ~150 Stripe Connect Express payout countries**; buyers are global apart from sanctioned jurisdictions. The sandbox is a separate account at `sandbox.polar.sh` with its own tokens and is CI-suitable, with one caveat: customer-facing emails there reach only members of your own Polar organization, so seat-invite flows need sub-addressed test addresses.
